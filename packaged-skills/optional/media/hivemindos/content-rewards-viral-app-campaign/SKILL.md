# Content Rewards Viral App Campaign

Turn an app into a performance-paid organic content machine: isolate the product result people want to see, make it easy for creators to reproduce in hundreds of variations, train creators with a brief that works like a course, and refresh formats from real performance.

## Core Contract

- Treat this as campaign design and operator guidance, not a promise of views, downloads, MRR, CPMs, or virality.
- Verify any current Content Rewards platform claim, audience size, CPM range, payout limit, case study, or launch URL before presenting it as current fact. If the user supplied the number, label it as user-supplied.
- Do not launch campaigns, spend money, sign creators, post content, schedule content, send DMs, or publish payout terms without explicit approval.
- Do not provide legal advice. For creator contracts, draft business terms for review by counsel and mark risky clauses.
- Keep content original. Analyze viral formats, but do not clone another creator's exact script, account identity, likeness, voice, or proprietary assets.
- Respect platform rules, music licensing, disclosure requirements, privacy law, and regulated-category claims. Flag health, finance, minors, body-image, dating, gambling, and before/after claims for review.
- If implementing payouts, affiliate tracking, creator accounts, or campaign dashboards, keep authoritative payment, entitlement, fraud, and quota decisions server-side. Local app state may display terms but must not be the source of truth for official value.

## Inputs

Ask only for missing essentials:

- App name, category, target user, and monetization model.
- The core action a user takes and the result the app produces.
- Proof assets: demo videos, screenshots, landing page, app store page, analytics, approved claims, or competitor examples.
- Target platforms: TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, X, Snapchat Spotlight, or other.
- Campaign route: Content Rewards agency, self-fulfilled Content Rewards campaign, own creator rewards program, or exploratory brief only.
- Payout constraints, max payout per post, affiliate bonuses, forbidden claims, required disclosures, and brand boundaries.

## Capability Discovery

Before going beyond pure strategy drafting, map available capabilities by intent:

- Current signal: browser/search, `hive-pulse`, platform creative centers, public ad libraries, or user-provided exports.
- Account or competitor analysis: public profile browsing, user-provided screenshots/exports, `instagram-reel-growth-workflow`, or similar short-form research skills.
- UGC production: `ai-ugc-production-pipeline`, `script-to-short`, image/video generation skills, stock-media skills, and render QA skills when assets are needed.
- Landing or influencer web version: project-specific frontend skills, `viral-product-landing-page`, and the repo's existing design/build patterns.
- Creator community operations: Discord or messaging skills only when the user asks to set up or operate a real community.
- Publishing/scheduling: `social-video-publishing` or another configured publisher with dry-run support, explicit approval, and provider receipts.

Mention missing capabilities by capability name. Never require one specific provider unless the user requested it.

## Workflow

### 1. Audit The Viral Aha Moment

Find the shortest visual sequence that proves the app does something people want:

```text
Input -> app action -> surprising/personalized result -> reason to share/pay
```

Score the moment:

- Clippable: understandable from a screen recording or 2-4 slide slideshow without explanation.
- Result-driven: the app produces a visible, personalized, ranked, improved, scored, redesigned, discovered, or emotionally charged output.
- Desire to know: people would pay or at least click to know their own result.
- Repeatable: creators can produce many variants without heavy editing.
- Controversy or tension: the result creates curiosity, ego, status, taboo, fear, relief, or identity-level stakes without crossing legal or platform lines.
- Conversion path: the viewer can imagine using the app immediately.

If the app lacks a strong aha moment, propose product changes before campaign tactics. Examples of stronger structures:

- Photo in, diagnostic or score out.
- Room/item/body/food/product in, improved design or recommendation out.
- User data in, personalized ranking or forecast out.
- Public object in, rarity, value, or hidden insight out.
- Messy state in, clean final version out.

### 2. Mine Viral Formats

Use recent public evidence when tools are available. For "trending", "current", "last 7 days", "last 30 days", or platform-specific claims, browse/search or say live access is unavailable.

Build a format table:

| Field | What to capture |
| --- | --- |
| Link | Source post, ad, or creator example. |
| Sound | Music/audio used, license/availability, and why it fits. |
| Hook | First-frame text or first spoken line. |
| Format | Slideshow, screen recording, direct-to-camera, stitch, storytime, ranking, challenge, reveal, etc. |
| Visuals | What the viewer sees in the first 3 seconds and at the reveal. |
| Editing notes | Template, captions, pacing, overlays, cuts, and rough production time. |
| Conversion logic | Why this would make a viewer install, try, subscribe, or share. |

Prefer formats that look native and simple. Low-production 2-slide slideshows, quick screen recordings, and rough creator posts often scale better than polished ads because more creators can reproduce them and they feel less like advertising.

Select 10-20 formats that are:

- easy to recreate in 30 seconds to 5 minutes
- tightly linked to the app's aha moment
- flexible across many niches, identities, locations, or languages
- clear enough that a new creator can copy the structure without copying the original post
- measurable through views, clicks, installs, conversions, comments, saves, or affiliate actions

### 3. Build The Content Bank

Create one format card per selected format. Each card should include:

- Format name.
- 3-10 example links or screenshots.
- Hook examples under 12 words.
- Music/audio links and allowed usage notes.
- Required app inputs and expected output.
- Caption ideas.
- Visual recipe: first frame, middle proof, reveal, CTA.
- AI prompts or editing prompts if the content uses generated assets.
- Example thumbnail or slideshow sequence.
- Do/don't notes specific to that format.
- Submission checklist and metric target.

Keep the bank practical enough that a new creator can open it and make a post immediately.

### 4. Write The Brief Like A Course

Do not create only a requirement list. Structure the creator brief as a training path:

1. Campaign overview: app, audience, approved claims, platforms, payout rules, max payout, deadlines, and support channels.
2. Contract and terms: creator rights, brand usage rights, content takedown/minimum-live terms, payment conditions, competing-app restrictions if applicable, account ownership terms if applicable, disclosure obligations, and review by counsel.
3. Account setup: profile picture, bio, handle patterns, link placement, warm-up behavior, posting cadence, and account safety.
4. Content creation modules: screen recordings, slideshow creation, AI asset generation, captions, music, overlays, and examples.
5. Format walkthroughs: video or step-by-step instructions for each top format.
6. Do's and don'ts: correct examples beside wrong examples.
7. Submission process: campaign link, post URL format, screenshots if needed, affiliate links, deadlines, dispute process, and payout timing.
8. Support and coaching: Discord channels, office hours, tickets, leaderboard, payout proof, and top-performing examples.

For contract drafts, output plain business terms for legal review. Do not imply the contract is enforceable without counsel.

### 5. Design The Influencer Web Version

Creators should not need to install the app, create an account, or expose private data just to make campaign content.

Specify a lightweight web version that:

- accepts safe sample or creator-provided inputs
- generates the app's aha-moment result quickly
- outputs a screenshot, short clip, transparent overlay, or slideshow frame
- supports many variants without creator engineering work
- contains approved copy and brand assets
- watermarks or labels outputs only when it helps campaign attribution
- avoids collecting unnecessary personal data
- uses server-side validation for paid or official results

If the user asks to build it, produce a compact product spec first: routes, states, input picker, output renderer, export buttons, abuse limits, analytics events, and privacy notes. Then build with the project's existing frontend patterns.

### 6. Choose Launch Route

Compare the launch route plainly:

| Route | Use when | Operator workload |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Content Rewards agency | The user wants help recruiting, briefing, managing creators, or scaling quickly. | Lower internal workload, less direct control. |
| Content Rewards self-fulfill | The user wants to run the campaign but use marketplace distribution. | Medium/high workload. |
| Own creator rewards program | The user wants full control or is not ready for Content Rewards. | Highest workload and more payout/fraud infrastructure. |

For self-fulfilled creator ops, design the Discord or community structure:

- Start-here, announcements, resources, format bank, do's/don'ts, submissions, support tickets, top performers, leaderboard, payout proof, and office-hours channels.
- Host regular coaching calls and collect repeated questions into the course.
- Show winning content and payouts to create social proof, but do not fake metrics.

### 7. Design Creator Rewards

Use incentives that reward both volume and business value:

- Base CPM or view payout, with max payout per post.
- Milestone bonuses, such as installs, qualified signups, purchases, subscriptions, or affiliate targets.
- Higher tiers for creators who consistently convert or follow the brief.
- Clear fraud, duplicate-content, paid-boost, deleted-post, and low-quality-content rules.
- Transparent payment timing and dispute process.

If actual money will move, require explicit approval and route through a proper payment system. Do not treat spreadsheets, local app state, or creator-submitted screenshots as authoritative payout proof without verification.

### 8. Enrich The Machine Weekly

Build a refresh loop:

1. Pull the week's top and bottom posts by views, retention, conversion, and payout efficiency.
2. Identify the winning hook, first frame, format, creator type, music, result type, and CTA.
3. Add winning examples to the content bank.
4. Retire confusing, low-converting, risky, or overused formats.
5. Generate 10 new hook variants and 3 new format tests.
6. Localize proven formats into new languages or regions only after checking cultural fit and claim/disclosure requirements.
7. Update creator training and announce the changes.

```text
Audit this app for a Content Rewards-style creator clipping campaign: [app details].
Find the strongest clippable aha moment, score it on clippability, desire to know, repeatability, controversy/tension, and conversion path, then propose product changes if the moment is too weak.
```

```text
Find 10-20 short-form viral formats for [app/category] from recent public examples.
```


## Quality Bar

- Tie every content format to a concrete app result, not generic virality advice.
- Keep creator instructions executable: links, steps, examples, templates, payout rules, and submission rules.
- Separate verified facts, user-supplied claims, and assumptions.
- Prefer simple, native-looking creative unless the evidence supports a higher-production format.
- Require approval before spend, posting, creator outreach, contract distribution, or payout changes.
