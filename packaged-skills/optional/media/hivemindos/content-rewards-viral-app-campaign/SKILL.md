# Content Rewards Viral App Campaign

Turn an app into a measured performance-paid content machine: prove the product moment, make it easy for creators to reproduce, verify the traffic, connect views to installs and revenue, and keep replacing decaying formats without scaling spend blindly.

## Workflow

### 0. Pass The Go/No-Go Gates

Do not confuse urgency with readiness. Score each gate 0-2 and show the evidence:

| Gate | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Visible app moment | Generic promise | Demonstrable with explanation | Understandable in one frame or short clip |
| Conversion path | Unmeasured | Installs or trials measured | Install through paid conversion measured |
| Retention/value | Unknown | Early proxy only | Cohort retention or contribution value observed |
| Attribution | Views only | Click/install link exists | Creator/post to downstream events reconciles |
| Creative evidence | No format evidence | A few plausible formats | One owned proof or a bounded discovery test |
| Risk controls | No cap or rights terms | Partial controls | Hard budget, payout cap, fraud, rights, and stop rules |

- Below 8/12: fix product, measurement, rights, or controls before scaling a roster.
- 8-10/12: run a small discovery cohort with a written maximum loss.
- 11-12/12: prepare controlled scaling, still with staged approvals.

Do not turn the source article's suggested cash balance, download-to-paid rate, or retention windows into universal gates. Calculate readiness from the app's own cash constraints, funnel, and payback.

### 1. Audit The Viral Aha Moment

Find the shortest visual sequence that proves the app does something people want:

```text
Input -> app action -> surprising/personalized result -> reason to share/pay
```

Score the moment:

- Clippable: understandable from a screen recording or 2-4 slide slideshow without explanation.
- Result-driven: the app produces a visible, personalized, ranked, improved, scored, redesigned, discovered, or emotionally charged output.
- Desire to know: people would pay or at least click to know their own result.
- Repeatable: creators can produce many variants without heavy editing.
- Controversy or tension: the result creates curiosity, ego, status, taboo, fear, relief, or identity-level stakes without crossing legal or platform lines.
- Conversion path: the viewer can imagine using the app immediately.

If the app lacks a strong aha moment, propose product changes before campaign tactics. Examples of stronger structures:

- Photo in, diagnostic or score out.
- Room/item/body/food/product in, improved design or recommendation out.
- User data in, personalized ranking or forecast out.
- Public object in, rarity, value, or hidden insight out.
- Messy state in, clean final version out.

### 2. Mine Viral Formats

Use recent public evidence when tools are available. For "trending", "current", "last 7 days", "last 30 days", or platform-specific claims, browse/search or say live access is unavailable.

Build a format table:

| Field | What to capture |
| --- | --- |
| Link | Source post, ad, or creator example. |
| Sound | Music/audio used, license/availability, and why it fits. |
| Hook | First-frame text or first spoken line. |
| Format | Slideshow, screen recording, direct-to-camera, stitch, storytime, ranking, challenge, reveal, etc. |
| Visuals | What the viewer sees in the first 3 seconds and at the reveal. |
| Editing notes | Template, captions, pacing, overlays, cuts, and rough production time. |
| Conversion logic | Why this would make a viewer install, try, subscribe, or share. |

Prefer formats that look native and simple. Low-production 2-slide slideshows, quick screen recordings, and rough creator posts often scale better than polished ads because more creators can reproduce them and they feel less like advertising.

Select 10-20 formats that are:

- easy to recreate in 30 seconds to 5 minutes
- tightly linked to the app's aha moment
- flexible across many niches, identities, locations, or languages
- clear enough that a new creator can copy the structure without copying the original post
- measurable through views, clicks, installs, conversions, comments, saves, or affiliate actions

### 3. Build The Content Bank

Create one format card per selected format. Each card should include:

- Format name.
- 3-10 example links or screenshots.
- Hook examples under 12 words.
- Music/audio links and allowed usage notes.
- Required app inputs and expected output.
- Caption ideas.
- Visual recipe: first frame, middle proof, reveal, CTA.
- AI prompts or editing prompts if the content uses generated assets.
- Example thumbnail or slideshow sequence.
- Do/don't notes specific to that format.
- Submission checklist and metric target.

Keep the bank practical enough that a new creator can open it and make a post immediately.

### 4. Write The Brief Like A Course

Do not create only a requirement list. Structure the creator brief as a training path:

1. Campaign overview: app, audience, approved claims, platforms, payout rules, max payout, deadlines, and support channels.
2. Contract and terms: creator rights, brand usage rights, content takedown/minimum-live terms, payment conditions, competing-app restrictions if applicable, account ownership terms if applicable, disclosure obligations, and review by counsel.
3. Account setup: profile picture, bio, handle patterns, link placement, warm-up behavior, posting cadence, and account safety.
4. Content creation modules: screen recordings, slideshow creation, AI asset generation, captions, music, overlays, and examples.
5. Format walkthroughs: video or step-by-step instructions for each top format.
6. Do's and don'ts: correct examples beside wrong examples.
7. Submission process: campaign link, post URL format, screenshots if needed, affiliate links, deadlines, dispute process, and payout timing.
8. Support and coaching: Discord channels, office hours, tickets, leaderboard, payout proof, and top-performing examples.

For contract drafts, output plain business terms for legal review. Do not imply the contract is enforceable without counsel.

### 5. Design The Influencer Web Version

Creators should not need to install the app, create an account, or expose private data just to make campaign content.

Specify a lightweight web version that:

- accepts safe sample or creator-provided inputs
- generates the app's aha-moment result quickly
- outputs a screenshot, short clip, transparent overlay, or slideshow frame
- supports many variants without creator engineering work
- contains approved copy and brand assets
- watermarks or labels outputs only when it helps campaign attribution
- avoids collecting unnecessary personal data
- uses server-side validation for paid or official results

If the user asks to build it, produce a compact product spec first: routes, states, input picker, output renderer, export buttons, abuse limits, analytics events, and privacy notes. Then build with the project's existing frontend patterns.

### 6. Choose Launch Route

Compare the launch route plainly:

| Route | Use when | Operator workload |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Content Rewards agency | The user wants help recruiting, briefing, managing creators, or scaling quickly. | Lower internal workload, less direct control. |
| Content Rewards self-fulfill | The user wants to run the campaign but use marketplace distribution. | Medium/high workload. |
| Own creator rewards program | The user wants full control or is not ready for Content Rewards. | Highest workload and more payout/fraud infrastructure. |

Choose between two test paths:

- **Owned-format scale:** the operator has already produced an original post that converts; use a small creator cohort to test whether the structure transfers.
- **Network discovery:** no proven format exists; cap the first cohort and budget explicitly, test several distinct format families, and treat the spend as research rather than scale.

For self-fulfilled creator ops, design the Discord or community structure:

- Start-here, announcements, resources, format bank, do's/don'ts, submissions, support tickets, top performers, leaderboard, payout proof, and office-hours channels.
- Host regular coaching calls and collect repeated questions into the course.
- Show winning content and payouts to create social proof, but do not fake metrics.

### 7. Select And Instrument The Creator Roster

Score creators on evidence that matches this campaign:

- Format-family proof: they have executed the same structural format, not merely posted in the category.
- Account health: organic history, plausible engagement, no obvious engagement-pod or bought-view pattern, and no policy red flags.
- Brief fidelity: follows required claims, disclosures, rights, naming, demo, CTA, and submission fields without hidden renegotiation.
- Throughput and consistency: can meet the agreed cadence without sacrificing compliance or originality.
- Editing fundamentals: first-frame clarity, dead-space removal, mobile-safe captions, hook stacking, and a readable app demo.
- Collaboration quality: responds to feedback, submits clean evidence, and surfaces new format ideas.

The source suggests at least one 500K-view hit, multiple daily posts, and 1-3 days of niche warm-up. Record these only as vendor heuristics. Replace them with campaign-specific minimums and never encourage spammy warm-up engagement or account manipulation.

Before the first paid view, define the authoritative submission record:

```text
creator -> account -> platform -> post -> format -> hook -> published at -> verified views -> clicks -> installs -> activations -> trials -> paid conversions -> revenue -> payout status -> fraud/appeal status
```

### 8. Design Creator Rewards

Use incentives that reward both volume and business value:

- Base CPM or view payout, with max payout per post.
- Milestone bonuses, such as installs, qualified signups, purchases, subscriptions, or affiliate targets.
- Higher tiers for creators who consistently convert or follow the brief.
- Clear fraud, duplicate-content, paid-boost, deleted-post, and low-quality-content rules.
- Transparent payment timing and dispute process.

Calculate the payout envelope before publishing terms:

```text
maximum creator payout per acquired customer
= conservative contribution value
- required profit buffer
- non-creator acquisition and service costs
```

Per-view pricing is acceptable only when invalid traffic is excluded and the operator still measures downstream value. The article's `$1-$3 per 1,000 verified views` is a vendor-claimed range, not a market rate. Set creator and campaign floors/caps that fit the app's own economics; disclose what happens to rejected views and how creators appeal.

If actual money will move, require explicit approval and route through a proper payment system. Do not treat spreadsheets, local app state, or creator-submitted screenshots as authoritative payout proof without verification.

### 9. Manage The Machine Daily And Weekly

Use one dashboard that can reconcile organic attention with business outcomes. At minimum track verified views, three-second hold or retention, clicks, store visits, installs, activation, trial, paid conversion, refunds, revenue, creator payout, cost per install/customer, and cohort retention. Comment quality is diagnostic, not proof of conversion.

Use this diagnostic ladder as a hypothesis map:

| Evidence pattern | First suspected bottleneck | Next controlled test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Healthy delivery but weak initial views/hold | Hook, first frame, format-audience fit, or account eligibility | Hold the body constant and test 3-5 hooks; check platform/account diagnostics before declaring a shadow ban |
| Initial attention but weak completion | Pacing, dead space, story tension, creator execution, or reveal timing | Recut the middle, move proof earlier, and compare creators on the same brief |
| Strong views but weak clicks/installs | App intent, demo clarity, CTA timing, caption naming, or offer mismatch | Add a truthful app demo and explicit next step; align the store promise |
| Installs but weak activation/payment | Onboarding, paywall, value delivery, trial terms, or product retention | Stop creative scaling and fix the downstream funnel |
| Paid customers but weak retention/refunds | Expectation mismatch or product value | Stop scale, review claims, and repair retention |

The article's `<1K`, `1K-10K`, and `100K+ without downloads` bands are source heuristics, not universal thresholds. Use the app's own distributions and downstream ratios to set bands.

Build a refresh loop:

1. Pull the week's top and bottom posts by views, retention, conversion, and payout efficiency.
2. Identify the winning hook, first frame, format, creator type, music, result type, and CTA.
3. Add winning examples to the content bank.
4. Retire confusing, low-converting, risky, or overused formats.
5. Allocate an explicit exploration slice while the winner runs. The article suggests 80% exploitation and 20% exploration; use that as a starting hypothesis and change it when decay speed or test cost says otherwise.
6. Test new hooks or format families in a small cohort before roster-wide rollout. The source suggests a 72-hour test and 10-30 creators per format; use observed sample size and conversion delay instead of treating either number as fixed.
7. Ask creators to submit interesting public formats with source links and an explanation of the transferable structure. Reward useful research without copying another creator's expression.
8. Localize proven formats only after checking cultural fit, music rights, claims, and disclosure rules.
9. Update creator training and announce the changed standard. Praise wins publicly; handle individual performance corrections privately.

### 10. Hand Rights-Cleared Winners Into Paid Testing

Organic performance is a discovery signal, not a paid-media verdict. Include rights-cleared organic winners and a bounded sample of strategically distinct underperformers in the paid test matrix; do not upload every clip without a spend cap.

Before handoff, require:

- documented paid-media permission and authorization duration from the creator
- a stable post/caption or approved editable asset
- current AI/sponsorship disclosures and policy review
- verified install and revenue attribution
- contribution-LTV ceiling, hard test-loss cap, and delayed-conversion window
- a human-approved launch card listing account, geography, budget, identity, creatives, and stop rules

For TikTok, use `tiktok-app-ads-growth-loop` to verify current Spark Ads, Smart+/manual, MMP, and scaling behavior. The article's `1.5x ROAS`, `$100/day`, and `15-20% weekly` figures are promotional heuristics; recalculate kill/hold/scale rules from contribution value and current platform guidance.

## Quality Bar

- Tie every content format to a concrete app result, not generic virality advice.
- Keep creator instructions executable: links, steps, examples, templates, payout rules, and submission rules.
- Separate verified facts, user-supplied claims, and assumptions.
- Label all figures inherited from the supplied RIZZ/AFNW guide as vendor-claimed and unverified unless independently confirmed from primary evidence.
- Prefer simple, native-looking creative unless the evidence supports a higher-production format.
- Diagnose views, retention, installs, payment, and retention as separate links; do not call a high-view campaign successful when it does not create attributable business value.
- Keep experimentation bounded: one changed variable, named cohort, maximum loss, review window, and explicit evidence required to expand.
- Publicly celebrate wins and standards; keep performance correction and offboarding private.
- Require approval before spend, posting, creator outreach, contract distribution, or payout changes.
