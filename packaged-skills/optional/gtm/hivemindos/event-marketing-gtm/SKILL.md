# Event Marketing GTM

Events treated as a pipeline engine, not a brand play. The system: lock 10–15 anchor attendees from existing relationships → activate each confirmed guest's network so the room fills itself → design the event for introductions → convert the warmth into calls and warm intros within 72 hours. The goal is never a full room; it is the right room — 20 perfectly matched ICP attendees out-produce 80 random ones.

**Hard rules:** anchors come from real existing relationships only — a cold contact framed as a trusted host connection is misrepresentation. Scarcity lines ("a couple of spots left") are used only when literally true. Network analysis of a guest's connections stays within publicly visible LinkedIn data and platform terms. AI drafts messages; a human reviews and sends every one. Channel-effectiveness and trigger-conversion figures circulating with this system are a vendor's own framework, not proven data.

## When events work

Run them when your ICP already attends industry events (strong signal: they post about events on LinkedIn) and your deal cycle is weeks-to-months — long enough for the relationship to develop. Skip or go virtual when the ICP is geographically dispersed; skip entirely when there is no follow-up system (goodwill with no revenue attached) or when the fill plan is cold email/DMs (cold outreach with an event wrapper and a catering bill).

Three principles: **anchor quality determines everything** (the first 10–15 set the gravitational pull), **every registrant is a distribution channel**, and **design for introductions, not attendance**.

## Anchors

An anchor is a decision-maker matching the ICP exactly, senior enough that peers want to share the room, whose LinkedIn network is mostly relevant ICP lookalikes (check connections *before* inviting), and with whom a real relationship exists — priority order: existing clients with results → active partners → strong warm relationships → warm intros from those groups. Cold anchors don't work.

The invite is short and specific, references the relationship or mutual, describes the **room** rather than the agenda ("about 15 people, all [ICP SEGMENT] leaders"), makes the invitee feel selected not solicited, and ends in one clear ask. No pitching — the invite's only job is making them want to be in the room. Start anchor outreach 6–8 weeks out so early confirmations become social proof for the next wave. Invite skeletons for existing clients, warm intros, and past attendees live in this repository's `workflows/gtm/events/templates.json` bank.

## +1 activation — the core mechanic

For each confirmed guest: scan their public LinkedIn network for ICP-fit peers, shortlist 2–3 specific names, and ask the guest to bring 1–2 as trusted +1s — then repeat on every new confirmation. People say yes because it is a value exchange: they look good bringing a peer into a curated room, the peer gets access, you get another qualified attendee. The compounding model: 5 anchors × 2 peers = 15 in the room; activate the new arrivals at +1 each = 25; one more pass ≈ 35–40 — all connected to someone already there, zero cold messages.

The ask always lands **after** confirmation, frames real scarcity naturally, gives the guest agency ("totally your call"), and works best with a specific name you already identified — specificity means they only say yes or no. A 15–20 second voice note outperforms a second DM for high-priority anchors. Ask templates and the six-step tracking loop are in the bank.

## Room design

The objective is a **live reference environment**: clients attending alongside peers from their own networks, so conversations start warm and prospects hear about you from peers they respect rather than from you. Never theatre-style seating — round tables or U-shapes under 20, with ICP attendees seated next to people they *should* know. Open with a structured intro round (name, company, one thing you're working on — 60 seconds each). Brief the room in 2–3 sentences, then hand it back; you are the reason they're together, not the centre of attention. One discussion question everyone can answer beats a slide deck. Pre-brief your team with one page: every attendee, who brought them, one context note each, the 2–3 priority conversations, and a starter for each.

## Post-event conversion

In-person warmth has a half-life — speed signals the conversation mattered. Same day: internal debrief (who spoke to whom, summary, hot/warm/cold, next step, follow-up owner) and thank-yous to anchors who brought +1s. Within 24–48h: a personal follow-up to every attendee (standard, +1-brought, and no-show variants in the bank). Within 72h: the warm-intro ask, while goodwill is at its peak — post-event ranks among the strongest lifecycle moments to ask, alongside positive NPS, late-stage deals, closed-won/lost, and end of onboarding (vendor framework, directionally sound).

Four rules of the intro ask: **specific never general** (one named person, so the answer is yes/no — never "know anyone who'd be a fit?"); **friction to zero** (provide the ready-to-forward intro email and a copyable pitch); **a real introduction, not passive permission** (a 3-way thread beats "mention my name"; if passive is all you get, act within the hour); **one ask at a time** (C-level at a strategic account = that intro only). After the intro lands, close the loop three times — meeting booked, first-call outcome, end of cycle win or lose. A referrer who hears nothing never refers again. If compensating, recognise effort over time; credibility outweighs incentive in B2B.

## Failure patterns

Most events die before doors open: non-ICP seat-filling (bar: "would I cold-email this person my core offer?"), no pre-event outreach (message every guest 5–7 days out naming 2–3 people they should meet), no intro architecture (map who meets whom and physically make the introductions — that is the host's job), pitching at the event (the follow-up is the ask, never the room), building the follow-up system after the event (warm conversations decay in ~72 hours), and cold contacts as anchors. All nine anti-patterns are in the bank.
