# Viral Product Principles

Use this reference for deep landing-page audits, rewrites, or teardown reports. These are intentionally sharp heuristics from the source material; apply them with product judgment and label pricing/paywall changes as experiments when evidence is missing.

## Offer

1. Avoid free plans by default when support, infrastructure cost, and roadmap distortion outweigh distribution value.
2. Test hard paywalls when validating willingness to pay matters more than signup volume.
3. Make pricing visible in navigation and on the page.
4. Use three pricing choices at most: good, better, best.
5. Consider one-time payments when a subscription is not structurally required.
6. Premium pricing can create talk value, but must be justified by a clear switch reason.

## First Screen

1. The hero should sell the product by itself because many visitors will not scroll.
2. Show the product, demo, output, or interaction before explaining it.
3. Use one CTA and make it describe the next action.
4. Make the headline simple enough for a fifth grader and memorable after 24 hours.
5. Sell one human desire, not a feature bundle.
6. Describe the product in under 10 words.

## Copy

1. Use numbers instead of adjectives.
2. Replace weak qualifiers with concrete claims where evidence supports them.
3. Write from customer language, not internal positioning.
4. Use copy only this product, founder, or audience could credibly say.
5. Show empathy before selling by describing the problem better than the customer can.
6. Use emotion when it fits: surprise, humor, relief, status, curiosity, or recognition.
7. Avoid names, puns, and invented words that require explanation.

## Page Structure

1. Reveal one idea per screen or section.
2. Be known for one thing; avoid making the page feel like a feature drawer.
3. Compare against competitors on buyer-relevant criteria.
4. Add testimonials or credible proof before sending serious traffic.
5. Let visitors try, inspect, or play with the strongest part of the product before buying when feasible.
6. Use a restrained palette: black text, white background, one accent color for the buy action is the extreme version of the rule.

## Shareability

1. Ride a wave people already discuss when the product genuinely fits the wave.
2. Do something or present something people have not seen before.
3. Put a visible founder voice or founder demo on the page when trust matters.
4. End with a footer or closing section people remember and want to share.
5. Treat the OG image like a YouTube thumbnail: click-worthy, legible, specific, and emotionally obvious.
