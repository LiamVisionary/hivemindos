# X Warm Outreach

Cold DMs on X mostly die in Message Requests — the folder nobody checks — because the sender is a stranger. The fix is sequencing, not copy: engage with a prospect's content for 10+ days until your name is familiar, then DM. The mechanism is concrete: follows and prior interactions change inbox routing (main inbox instead of Message Requests), and name recognition is what gets a message read. Value delivered before the ask is what gets it answered.

**Hard rules:** X's platform-manipulation rules prohibit automated likes, replies, and DMs — every engagement here is a human action or a human-approved draft, and tools that auto-engage on your behalf are an account-loss risk that also poisons the authenticity the ladder depends on. Uplift figures circulating for warm-vs-cold methods are vendor marketing claims, not proven results. Silence after the single follow-up means stop.

## The 14-day ladder

**Days 1–3 — get on their radar.** Build a list of 20–50 leads from competitor followers, the influencers your ICP already reads, and people tweeting about the problems you solve. Qualify each: active in the last 7 days, ICP fit from bio and content, and they actually engage with replies. Then start small: 2–3 thoughtful likes per person (never a burst of 10), and on day 3 one genuine reply each on your best 10 — add to their point, share a real experience, or ask a smart question. "Great post!" is invisible.

**Days 4–7 — become a familiar name.** 5–10 quality replies per day across the list, mixed with likes-only days so it stays natural. Three reply shapes carry the whole phase: the **add-on** (a point they didn't make), the **story** (a quick real experience with a concrete result), and the **question** (one that shows you thought past the tweet). Around day 6 — once your name has shown up 5–8 times — follow them; they'll check your profile, so it must already say who you help and show recent valuable posts.

**Days 8–10 — deepen with the warmest.** Narrow to the 10–20 who liked back, replied, or followed back. Like their posts soon after they publish, keep the replies thoughtful, quote-tweet their best content with your own take. For those who engaged back, send the day-10 soft touch: a conversation starter that references their specific work and asks a genuine question — no pitch, no ask.

**Days 11–14 — the DM.** Three lines: acknowledge them (specifically, not creepily), say why you're reaching out, ask one simple question. Then wait two days without double-messaging. On day 14, one casual follow-up with an explicit easy exit ("no worries if it's not relevant — say the word and I'll stop"), or the one-word nudge: their name and a question mark. The easy out is the mechanism — respecting their time is what earns the reply.

## The three DM shapes

1. **Observation** — proof of attention plus a yes/no question: "Noticed you're [building X]. Are you doing any [relevant motion] right now, or focused elsewhere?"
2. **Value lead** — a specific result plus an easy out: "We've been testing something relevant — getting [result] with [method]. Worth hearing about, or not a focus right now?"
3. **Direct question** — simplest, both answers open doors: "Are you happy with how you're [doing the job] right now, or is that something you're trying to improve?"

## Why it works, and what it costs

The DM lands in the main inbox, the name is recognized, value came before the ask, and trust preceded the pitch. The cost is real — roughly 1–2 focused hours a day of finding, reading, and replying — and that cost is the moat: the effort is visible to the prospect and cannot be automated without becoming spam.
