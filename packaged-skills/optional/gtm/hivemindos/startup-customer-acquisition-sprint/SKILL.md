# Startup Customer Acquisition Sprint

## Purpose

Use this skill to turn a product into a high-volume weekly customer acquisition loop. The bias is action: launch repeatedly, create proof in public, contact warm leads, borrow competitor distribution, seed creator content, buy targeted attention, and measure what converts.

Treat the source playbook as an aggressive operator heuristic, not a guarantee. Preserve trust, platform rules, consent, paid endorsement disclosure, and privacy.

## Inputs To Collect

Ask only for missing information that changes execution:

- Product URL or concise product description.
- Ideal customer profile, including who should not be targeted.
- Primary conversion goal: waitlist, demo call, install, trial, paid customer, or retained active user.
- Geography, language, budget, founder availability, and any compliance limits.
- Existing assets: demo videos, landing page, customer proof, social accounts, competitors, email domain, CRM.

If the user wants speed and has not specified details, make reasonable assumptions and label them.

## Core Weekly Loop

Create a one-week sprint with seven lanes. Each lane should produce concrete artifacts, owners, quantities, and a scoreboard.

### 1. Launch-Max

Launch at least three times with different angles instead of treating launch as a one-day event.

- Build a launch list: Product Hunt, Hacker News, Dev Hunt, BetaList, Peerlist, Indie Hackers, relevant subreddits, niche directories, newsletters, Discords, Slacks, and local startup communities.
- For each launch, choose a distinct hook: technical demo, founder story, use-case teardown, open-source angle, customer result, contrarian market point, or limited offer.
- Prepare reusable launch assets: short title, one-sentence promise, demo clip, screenshots, founder comment, FAQ, first-comment link stack, and follow-up post.
- Track visits, signups, replies, calls booked, customers, and qualitative objections by launch surface.

### 2. Competitor Backlink And Directory Cloning

Find where competitors already get qualified attention, then get listed in the same or better places.

- Identify 3-10 direct or adjacent competitors.
- Pull strong backlinks, directory listings, comparison pages, listicles, templates, podcasts, newsletters, and resource pages using available SEO tools or public search operators.
- Prioritize pages by relevance, traffic intent, freshness, and likelihood of accepting updates.
- Create a better asset than the competitor reference: clearer comparison, more current screenshots, free tool, benchmark, tutorial, template, or buyer guide.
- Draft polite outreach asking the owner to add, update, or supplement the resource. Do not ask for deceptive replacement or make unsupported claims.

### 3. Warm Outbound From Social Engagement

Convert build-in-public attention into conversations.

- Review people who liked, replied to, reposted, bookmarked, subscribed, or visited through first-party analytics.
- Use permitted exports, platform tools, CRM enrichment, or manual review. Do not bypass access controls, scrape private data, or automate spam.
- Filter for fit against the ideal customer profile before messaging.
- Send short, personal messages tied to the specific post or pain. The goal is a conversation, not a blast.
- Cap daily sends to a level the founder can handle with human follow-up.

### 4. UGC Creator Seeding

Recruit niche creators to test fresh angles and social proof.

- Find 20-30 small or mid-sized creators on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts, X, or LinkedIn who already speak to the ICP.
- Prioritize creators with audience fit, believable use cases, recent posting consistency, comment quality, and low production friction.
- Offer a fixed test fee plus clear performance incentives when budget allows.
- Provide a brief with the product promise, allowed claims, demo steps, disclosure requirements, prohibited claims, and tracking link.
- Keep content authentic: disclose compensation where required and never present paid content as an independent organic review.

### 5. Video-First Build In Public

Use short demos more than screenshots or text when the product can be shown.

- Post use-case videos on X, LinkedIn, TikTok, Reels, Shorts, and relevant communities.
- Make each video demonstrate one concrete before/after, not a generic feature tour.
- Use repeatable formats: "I tried X so you do not have to", "before/after workflow", "watch me build/fix/automate", "one weird use case", "competitor alternative", "customer teardown".
- End with one clear next action: comment keyword, try link, book call, join waitlist, or DM.

### 6. Customer Watering Holes

Find where buyers already spend attention and buy access carefully.

- Map the ICP's Slack groups, Discords, subreddits, newsletters, podcasts, YouTube channels, LinkedIn creators, X accounts, events, job boards, and tools marketplaces.
- Separate places for listening, participating, posting, sponsorship, and direct partnership.
- Buy shoutouts or sponsorships only where audience fit and past performance are credible.
- Adapt the offer to the venue's norms. Lead with useful content or a concrete demo, not generic promotion.

### 7. Trend Capture

Every week, find viral formats in the niche and fold the product into the conversation.

- Search for recent high-engagement posts in the category.
- Extract formats, hooks, objections, screenshots, memes, demos, and reply patterns that are working now.
- Reply, quote, and post original versions with the product naturally embedded.
- Avoid forcing the product into unrelated trends or copying another creator's work too closely.

## Weekly Output Format

When asked to plan or run the sprint, return:

1. **Assumptions**: ICP, offer, goal, time budget, spend budget.
2. **North-star target**: one weekly customer goal and the leading indicators needed to reach it.
3. **Seven-lane plan**: actions, quantities, assets to create, tools/data needed, and owner.
4. **Message pack**: 3 launch angles, 3 outbound messages, 3 creator briefs, and 5 video hooks when relevant.
5. **Scoreboard**: a table with lane, activity count, replies, calls, signups, paid customers, cost, and notes.
6. **Next review trigger**: what result would cause doubling down, changing angle, or stopping a lane.

## Quality Bar

- Specific beats comprehensive. A usable list of 50 targets beats a generic lecture.
- Prefer weekly quotas and drafts over strategy-only advice.
- Tie every tactic to the ICP and conversion goal.
- Include retention warning: once acquisition works, identify the activation and retention metric that proves customers stay.
- If claims depend on live trends, current backlinks, current launch surfaces, or current creator rates, verify with current sources or label them as assumptions.

## Guardrails

- Do not recommend illegal scraping, credential misuse, platform evasion, fake reviews, undisclosed ads, harassment, or bulk spam.
- Respect opt-outs and do-not-contact signals.
- Avoid collecting or storing unnecessary personal data. Keep lead lists limited to business-relevant fields and a clear reason for contact.
- For regulated products, add claim review before launches, creator briefs, outbound, and paid placements.
