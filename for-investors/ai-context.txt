# HivemindOS Commercial Sequence

HivemindOS grows one customer relationship in stages. Each stage opens only when the one before it shows real demand. The product stays useful without a subscription, credits, a marketplace, or a token.

```text
local adoption
  -> first managed service
  -> recurring cloud subscription
  -> managed usage expansion
  -> agent transaction expansion
  -> enterprise controls
  -> marketplace liquidity
```

## Stage one: free local adoption

People build and run local agents, workflows, models, memory, and apps on their own machines with their own provider keys. This free product is how customers arrive and why they trust HivemindOS. It is not weakened to push cloud sales.

## Stage two: first paid activation

The first purchases are concrete and pay-as-you-go:

- publish an app at a stable URL
- keep one agent running after your own machines turn off
- use a managed model or API without opening another provider account
- run a hosted MiroShark simulation from the shared HivemindOS balance or direct x402 (local simulation stays free)

The hosted MiroShark simulation is $1.20 per accepted run. That is the complete customer price, and a run that does not start is not charged.

## Stage three: Hivemind Cloud subscription

Cloud Pro and Cloud Team charge for the persistent operating layer: schedules, monitoring, shared history, approvals, spending controls, deployment history, shared agents and memory, and audit trails. Managed infrastructure stays metered, because its cost grows with agent activity, not with seats.

## Stage four: Agent Economy Transaction Expansion

As agents get budgets and do more economic work, HivemindOS earns disclosed fees on supported trading, payment, and routing actions.

| Economic action | Current HivemindOS economics |
| --- | ---: |
| DEX swap | 0.20% platform fee |
| Supported live stock or tokenized stock execution | 0.10% execution fee |
| Paid x402 or Veil private-payment execution | 0.50% platform fee |
| Bankr-managed Base copy trade | 0.50% post-verification fee, $0.02–$0.50 |
| Eligible Hyperliquid perp fill | 0.005% builder fee |
| Qualifying Base x402 transaction | Builder Code attribution; rewards are contingent rather than guaranteed |

This engine should grow only while execution stays competitive, fees stay visible, and settlement success stays high. Ordinary wallet transfers and revenue earned outside HivemindOS stay free. Other Bankr actions (swaps, cross-chain actions, token launches, prediction markets, NFTs, automations) carry no HivemindOS fee; only hosted copy trading does, after each copied swap is verified.

The full rail list, with Robinhood Chain and minimums, is on the [investor overview](index.html#transaction-fees).

## Stage five: enterprise

Enterprise is the same control plane with stronger deployment, identity, policy, reliability, and support commitments. It is sold only for capabilities that exist: private deployment, SSO, audit export, regional controls, and SLAs are each labeled available, pilot, or planned.

## Stage six: marketplaces

An agent, workflow, or compute marketplace launches only after there is recurring buyer demand and suppliers can expect real utilization. Fees scale with what HivemindOS provides:

| HivemindOS role | Fee policy |
| --- | ---: |
| External revenue with no HivemindOS involvement | 0% |
| Billing or settlement only | Small disclosed processing/platform fee |
| HivemindOS sources the buyer | 5–10% target |
| HivemindOS sources, hosts, executes, supports, and protects the transaction | 10–20% target |

GMV, provider earnings, and buyer spend are reported separately from net platform revenue. The compute marketplace has its own [evidence gates](paid-features/hive-compute-marketplace.html).

## Commercial gates

Expansion is evidence-gated:

| Gate | Evidence required |
| --- | --- |
| Self-serve subscription launch | Repeated paid activation and retained managed usage |
| Transaction-engine expansion | Repeated agent economic activity, competitive execution, visible fees, low failure rates, and positive contribution margin |
| Team expansion | Multi-user demand for approvals, budgets, and shared history |
| Enterprise selling | A repeatable enterprise use case plus security and support readiness |
| Agent/workflow marketplace | Retained buyers seeking reusable supply |
| Compute marketplace expansion | Sufficient demand to create provider utilization |
| HIVE utility changes | Product-market fit, specialist legal review, server-authoritative benefit definitions, and exact prospective public terms |

## Metrics that matter

- activated and product-qualified workspaces
- paid conversion by activation path
- 30-day and 90-day paid retention
- recurring revenue per paid workspace
- managed usage gross profit
- fee-bearing agent transaction volume by rail
- realized net take rate and transaction-fee revenue
- successful fee settlement and post-action collection rate
- Base Builder Code-attributed transaction count and rewards actually earned
- verified successful agent outcomes per paid workspace
- support and infrastructure cost per workspace
- contribution margin
- expansion versus contraction

Payment volume, deployed agents, token activity, and marketplace GMV are supporting metrics. They do not replace net revenue, take rate, contribution margin, or retained customers.

# HIVE × Robinhood Chain Bridge

**Community review draft — July 20, 2026**

We are considering bringing HIVE from Base to Robinhood Chain without creating a second supply or asking the community to fund a new liquidity pool. This draft explains the design so the Base and HIVE communities can challenge it before any mainnet launch.

In short:

- Base remains HIVE's home market.
- HIVE on Robinhood Chain would be backed 1:1 by HIVE locked on Base.
- No separate, unbacked supply would be minted.
- No HIVE/ETH pool on Robinhood Chain would be needed at launch.
- Robinhood users could still buy or sell HIVE through the existing Base market.

## How the bridge works

The canonical HIVE contract stays on Base:

```text
Network: Base
Contract: 0xA382c83e2a3B79368f372c2EB9b6925ffAf45bA3
Initial supply: 100,000,000,000 HIVE
```

Moving HIVE to Robinhood Chain:

1. Your HIVE is locked in a bridge contract on Base.
2. The same amount is created for you on Robinhood Chain.

Moving it back:

1. The Robinhood HIVE is destroyed.
2. The matching Base HIVE is unlocked for you.

This is a lock-and-mint bridge. Every spendable HIVE on Robinhood Chain has one HIVE locked on Base, so usable supply is never duplicated.

## Where the liquidity comes from

The bridge only moves HIVE someone already owns; it needs no trading pool. Buying and selling would use HIVE's existing Base market instead of a thin second market.

**Buying from Robinhood Chain:**

```text
User pays ETH or USDG on Robinhood Chain
              ↓
Relay routes the payment to Base
              ↓
The existing Base market sells HIVE
              ↓
The purchased HIVE is bridged back to the user on Robinhood Chain
```

The HIVE comes from sellers already in the Base market, not from a HivemindOS treasury wallet.

**Selling from Robinhood Chain:**

```text
User bridges HIVE back to Base
              ↓
The Base market buys the HIVE
              ↓
Relay returns ETH or USDG to the user on Robinhood Chain
```

At first there would be no local Robinhood HIVE market. Robinhood wallets could hold and transfer HIVE, while trades use Base liquidity through a guided cross-chain flow.

## Proposed fees

The current testnet design uses:

- **0.05% direct bridge fee** when HIVE crosses chains.
- **0.45% routed-trade app fee** when the app coordinates a buy or sell through Relay.
- **0.50% nominal HivemindOS fee** for a routed trade that includes one bridge step.

Relay costs, gas, price impact, and provider fees are separate. You would see the complete quote before signing.

The bridge contract permanently caps its own fee at 0.25%. Governance could lower the fee but could not raise it above that cap without replacing the contracts and a new public review. These are proposed launch fees, not a promise that every trade costs exactly 0.50% all-in.

## What has been tested

The direct bridge is deployed on Base Sepolia and Robinhood testnet. A real round trip:

- 1,000 test HIVE moved from Base Sepolia to Robinhood testnet.
- 999.5 HIVE arrived after the 0.05% bridge fee.
- The HIVE was bridged back to Base Sepolia.
- Afterward, the remaining Robinhood supply exactly matched the net HIVE locked on Base.
- Contract, invariant, recovery, hosted-gateway, and browser tests passed.

Relay supports Base and Robinhood Chain on mainnet, and a read-only quote found a route from Robinhood ETH into the Base HIVE market. Relay does **not** support Robinhood testnet, so the full buy/sell flow was tested only with controlled simulations. It must pass a small, capped mainnet trial before wider use.

No HIVE mainnet bridge contract has been deployed, and no real mainnet funds have moved.

## Safeguards

- No owner-controlled mint function for Robinhood HIVE.
- Every Robinhood HIVE must stay backed by locked Base HIVE.
- Hourly and daily limits cap how much can move in either direction.
- A guardian can pause the bridge but cannot mint, withdraw backing, or change configuration.
- Mainnet governance changes must wait at least 72 hours.
- Bridge fees are tracked separately from backing, so governance cannot withdraw user principal as "fees."
- The app accepts only the official HIVE and payment-token addresses.
- Expired quotes, wrong wallets, wrong networks, duplicate submissions, and excessive price impact are refused.
- The direct bridge is separate from Relay, so a Relay outage cannot make bridged HIVE unbacked.

## Risks

- **Bridge risk:** depends on LayerZero messaging, its verification network, and our bridge contracts.
- **Routing risk:** one-click trading depends on Relay continuing to support both chains.
- **Market risk:** large trades still move the HIVE price on Base. A bridge does not create liquidity or guarantee an exit price.
- **Multi-step risk:** a routed trade spans several transactions. The app can save and resume each step, but separate blockchains cannot be made truly atomic.
- **Governance risk:** pausing, limits, fee withdrawals, and configuration need transparent multisig and timelock operations.
- **New-chain risk:** Robinhood Chain has less production history than Base.

The bridge should launch with conservative limits, public monitoring, and a small real-money trial, not unlimited capacity on day one.

## Questions for the community

1. Does keeping all price discovery and liquidity on Base make sense for the first launch?
2. Is 0.05% for direct bridging and 0.50% nominally for routed trades reasonable?
3. Should ETH and USDG both be supported for Robinhood-side buying and selling?
4. What trade-size and price-impact limits would you want at launch?
5. What additional audits, monitors, multisig participants, or emergency controls would earn your trust?
6. Would you prefer direct bridging to launch first, with routed trading enabled only after a separate trial?

# HIVE Staking And Community Tiers

HIVE staking is optional membership. Stake HIVE to reach one of six bee tiers, which earn Honey faster, give more free-agent usage, and unlock member pricing of up to 61% off on participating services. HivemindOS works fully without holding or staking HIVE.

## What staking gives you

- wallet-linked community identity and profile status
- a Honey earning multiplier
- a free-agent usage multiplier
- member pricing of up to 61% off on participating services
- eligibility for token-specific community activities

Staking does not grant company ownership, binding governance, treasury control, admin access, guaranteed placement, yield, revenue share, or a financial return. The benefits are services, not payouts. Cloud subscriptions, managed usage, and enterprise contracts can all be bought without HIVE.

## How to stake and unstake

The staking contract on Base is non-custodial:

1. Connect a Base-compatible wallet.
2. Approve and stake HIVE. The contract records your active stake, which sets your tier.
3. To leave, request unstaking.
4. Wait for the visible three-day cooldown.
5. Withdraw your HIVE to the same wallet.

You can always withdraw your principal after the cooldown. Staked HIVE is not company treasury property and cannot be used for buybacks.

## The six tiers

| Public tier | Active stake | Honey earning | Free-agent usage |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Honeybee | 1m HIVE | 1.00× | 1.10× |
| Bumblebee | 10m HIVE | 1.10× | 1.20× |
| Mason Bee | 50m HIVE | 1.25× | 1.35× |
| Orchid Bee | 100m HIVE | 1.45× | 1.50× |
| Carpenter Bee | 250m HIVE | 1.70× | 1.75× |
| Queen Bee | 1b HIVE | 2.00× | 2.00× |

These are membership tiers, not investment-return tiers. Tier names and thresholds do not promise future value.

## Honey earning multipliers

Your tier multiplies Honey only when you earn it from accepted contribution evidence. Holding HIVE alone creates no Honey. Honey stays one non-transferable, non-spendable record; a multiplier changes how much an accepted contribution records, but it does not make Honey cash or convert it to HIVE. See [Honey, HIVE, And Treasury](honey-hive-treasury.html#honey).

## Free-Agent Usage Multipliers

Everyone gets the ordinary free-agent allowance, powered by Swarm Sovereign models. A workspace with a signature-verified link to a staking wallet gets its tier's multiplier on its daily request and token allowance.

- The allowance is a service benefit. It cannot be transferred, redeemed, withdrawn, sold, or converted into cash, HIVE, Honey, revenue, or treasury assets.
- One verified workspace shares one boosted allowance across all its devices.
- IP and platform-wide safety limits remain unchanged.
- If the tier check fails for any reason, you get the ordinary unstaked allowance.
- If your lifetime Honey qualifies you for a higher multiplier, the higher one applies. They do not stack.

The quota schedule may change or pause going forward for capacity, abuse, legal, security, or operational reasons.

## Member Pricing: Up To 61% Off

Participating services may offer HIVE members up to 61% off. This is consumption pricing: a member pays less for eligible usage, and no tier receives a payout.

Exact service prices, cost floors, eligible products, and tier-level discount calculations remain server-owned. The hosted service checks your stake and quotes the price; the app cannot grant a tier, change a price, or go below a cost floor. "Up to 61% off" is the maximum, not a promise for every product or tier. The exact price is shown when you get a quote.

## Revenue Policy Is Separate From Staking

HivemindOS allocates 15% of each service's server-owned allocation basis (realized margin for most managed services, recognized platform revenue for others) to HIVE buybacks. A separate exact 15% goes to the company treasury. The treasury does not fund buybacks, and staked principal funds neither. Confirmed purchases are published in the [HIVE Buyback Ledger](https://hivemindos.app/buybacks/). HivemindOS does not burn HIVE.

Buybacks and treasury allocations give holders and stakers no payout, ownership, governance right, price promise, or claim. See [Revenue Allocations And Treasury Boundaries](treasury-reserve-and-seasonal-programs.html).

## Changes to tiers and benefits

HivemindOS shows stakers the contract address, chain, active stake, pending unstake, cooldown, and withdrawal state. It does not market staking with expected price gains, and core product access never depends on holding HIVE.

Tier thresholds, member pricing, eligibility, and benefits may change, pause, or end for legal, regulatory, tax, accounting, security, liquidity, market-integrity, reserve, or operational reasons. We publish material benefit or policy changes prospectively with an effective date, and completed on-chain receipts stay on the public record. See [Tokenomics Policy Changes](honey-hive-treasury.html#tokenomics-policy-changes).

# Honey, HIVE, And Treasury

HivemindOS has three separate things that are never one balance: Honey, Hivemind Cloud credits, and HIVE. This page explains each one and how revenue is allocated.

| Layer | Purpose | What it is not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Honey | One cumulative, non-transferable, non-spendable record of verified ecosystem contribution and bounded recognition, with auditable source labels | Cash, purchased credit, ownership, or promised token value |
| Hivemind Cloud credits | Purchased, spend-only service value for managed infrastructure | An investment, reward token, or cash-equivalent wallet |
| HIVE | An optional ecosystem token and payment rail where supported | Company equity, a required product toll, or a guaranteed claim on revenue |

## Honey

Honey records useful, verified participation, such as reviewed agent work, accepted open-source contributions, testing, documentation, or completed bounties. Raw model tokens, self-reported activity, or unobserved output do not earn Honey.

How Honey is earned:

- **Community Hive Brain:** 0.1 Honey for each distinct copy-trading outcome that passes hosted verification. Unverified, duplicate, and retried submissions earn nothing. The contributing wallet must link to a HivemindOS workspace; until it does, the reward waits as pending.
- **Telegram recognition:** members with a signature-verified identity can give a fixed, scarce Honey recognition for a named contribution. Daily, pair, recipient, cooldown, and global budget limits apply.
- **Historical HIVE tips:** past tip-receiver rank may be recorded at 1 Honey per 1,000,000 HIVE received.

Source labels (mission, verified work, peer recognition, learning, historical seed) show where Honey came from. They do not create different kinds of Honey.

What Honey does:

- **More free-agent usage.** Lifetime Honey raises a workspace's daily free-agent request and token allowance, from 1.10× at 10 Honey to a capped 2.00× at 5,000. This allowance and a HIVE staking allowance do not stack; the higher one applies. It is revocable service access, not stored value or a withdrawal right.
- **Staking multiplies Honey.** An active HIVE staking tier multiplies qualifying Honey earned: 1.00×, 1.10×, 1.25×, 1.45×, 1.70×, and 2.00× from Honeybee through Queen Bee.

HivemindOS-controlled infrastructure calculates Honey totals and tiers from verified records. Local state cannot grant Honey, a tier, or contribution evidence.

Honey does not automatically convert to HIVE. Honey-to-HIVE exchange and claim routes stay off unless a separately authorized hosted policy turns them on, and the product never shows an unavailable conversion as "ready to claim."

## Hivemind Cloud credits

Credits are how customers pay for managed services: managed agents, hosted models and APIs, app publishing, managed media and tools, and other HivemindOS-operated services.

Credits are spend-only, nontransferable, not redeemable for cash, recorded on the server, and funded only by verified payments. Prices and balances are shown in ordinary dollars, not as token rewards.

## HIVE

HIVE may support optional community identity, payments, staking-based Honey earning multipliers, free-agent usage multipliers, member pricing of up to 61% off on participating services, marketplace settlement, or bounties, where those features are available and legally reviewed. Exact service prices remain server-owned.

You do not need HIVE to run HivemindOS locally, buy Hivemind Cloud services, join a paid workspace, use enterprise features, or bring a provider key. HIVE is not company ownership. See [HIVE Staking And Community Tiers](hive-staking-and-community-tiers.html) for tier details.

## Revenue allocation

Customer collections are not automatically platform revenue. Recognized platform revenue excludes pass-through costs, refunds, and reversals. Marketplace gross merchandise value is not platform revenue; only HivemindOS's earned fee counts. Purchased credits are recognized only when consumed.

Each paid service has a server-owned allocation basis, published in the official commercial catalog: realized margin for most managed services, recognized platform revenue for others. From that basis:

```text
server-owned per-service allocation basis
├─ 15% -> HIVE buybacks
├─ 15% -> company treasury
└─ remainder -> outside the tokenomics allocation, with no fixed use assigned here
```

- HivemindOS allocates 15% of each service's server-owned allocation basis to HIVE buybacks.
- A separate exact 15% of the same server-owned allocation basis goes to the company treasury.
- The treasury does not fund HIVE buybacks. Each rail has its own accounting and receipts.
- No user wallet, staked principal, local setting, or client revenue claim can choose either amount or recipient.
- Confirmed purchases are published in the [HIVE Buyback Ledger](https://hivemindos.app/buybacks/). Pending or failed purchases do not count. HivemindOS does not burn HIVE.
- Hive Research's first paid production run produced one confirmed HIVE purchase on Base under its earlier app-specific policy. That receipt stays public; new Hive Research revenue follows the platform-wide 15% buyback allocation.

The treasury allocation remains company property. It creates no holder or staker ownership, redemption right, revenue share, governance right, or claim on treasury assets. Buybacks do not promise a token price, liquidity, profit, or return. More detail: [Revenue Allocations And Treasury Boundaries](treasury-reserve-and-seasonal-programs.html).

## Tokenomics Policy Changes

Tokenomics is an operating policy, not an immutable promise. Allocation formulas, buyback amounts and timing, staking benefits, tier thresholds, and eligibility may change, pause, or end when needed for legal, regulatory, tax, accounting, corporate, security, liquidity, market-integrity, reserve, provider, or operational reasons.

Material changes are published with an effective date and apply going forward unless the law requires otherwise. # App Builder And Hosting

HivemindOS lets users ask an agent to build and refine an app on their own hardware, then pay for hosting only when the app needs to be shared or stay online.

- **App Builder drives adoption.** Building, file edits, installs, and local previews run on the user's own or linked machines, with no managed cloud agent needed.
- **Hive Publish drives recurring revenue.** HivemindOS runs the public URL, release storage, access rules, renewals, and isolated server-side runtime.

For the user workflow and limits, see the [App Builder guide](../../for-users/features/app-builder.html).

## Prices

| Revenue stream | Customer value | Current charge |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Share Preview | A branded URL for a finished static build without an ongoing commitment | **$1 for seven days** |
| Hosted Site | Renewable static hosting for ordinary websites | **$5 per 30 days** |
| Pro Site | Renewable static hosting with a larger release allowance | **$15 per 30 days** |
| Dynamic App | Renewable isolated server-side edge execution | **$25 per 30 days** |

- Each charge covers one Site. A customer can host several Sites.
- Anyone can buy hosting, including users who build entirely on their own machine. They don't need a Managed Cloud Agent.
- Renewable plans have opt-in auto-renew and debit the hosted credit balance every 30 days.
- After a failed renewal, a seven-day grace period keeps the app online, and a 30-day retention window lets the customer recover the release.
- This is renewable hosting, not lifetime hosting.
- The $1 preview is meant to come from an existing credit balance or a larger top-up, so a card fee doesn't eat a $1 charge.

## Role in the business

Publishing is an easy first purchase: a free local user pays for one managed outcome before needing a subscription. Retained publishers can then grow into Cloud Pro, Cloud Team, managed agents, models, and APIs. Hosting is an entry point into Hivemind Cloud, not a separate business.

## Costs and margin protection

HivemindOS charges per Site, while the hosting provider's base plan and included allowances are shared across all Sites. Cloudflare's current pricing starts with a **$5 monthly account minimum**, then meters extra requests and CPU. See Cloudflare's [Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/) and [Dynamic Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dynamic-workers/pricing/). Storage, logs, support, payment processing, refunds, and taxes vary with real use, so gross margin is measured from production cohorts, not estimated.

To cap cost, each Dynamic App Site is limited to 10 million requests, 250 million reserved CPU-ms, 1 million storage operations, and 1 GiB of runtime storage. Every request reserves the full 25ms CPU ceiling, so the budget is conservative. When the request or CPU limit is reached, requests are refused until the allowance resets. When the storage-operation allowance runs out, further storage calls are refused, and a write that would exceed storage capacity fails without changing the stored value. At current Cloudflare overage rates, a Site using its full allowance stays bounded relative to its $25 plan price.

Investor reporting should show hosted properties, preview-to-renewal conversion, 30/90-day hosting retention, revenue per property, infrastructure, payment, and support cost, and contribution margin.

Official prices, renewals, credit debits, and URL ownership are enforced by HivemindOS servers. The app can show and submit a plan, but it cannot change hosting prices.

# Hive Compute Marketplace

Hive Compute is an experimental option, not a near-term revenue assumption. The near-term business is Hivemind Cloud: subscriptions, metered managed execution, and the Managed Agent Ops pilot. No marketplace revenue, provider supply, or network effect is forecast until real demand and reliable supply exist.

Users can already try self-hosted compute in the app; see [Hive Compute](../../for-users/features/hive-compute.html).

## Why it is deferred

A two-sided compute marketplace means running several businesses at once:

- finding buyers and routing their work
- finding hosts and checking their quality
- balances, metering, settlement, and payouts
- fraud, disputes, sanctions, tax, and support
- reliability, privacy, attestation, and reputation

These only pay off once Hivemind Cloud has repeatable demand. Building them too early would pull focus from the core business.

## Evidence Gates

The marketplace launches only when all of these are true:

1. At least 20 paying Hivemind Cloud customers with recurring managed-compute usage.
2. At least $25,000 in monthly managed-compute gross merchandise value for three consecutive months.
3. At least 10 qualified supply partners that meet measured latency, uptime, privacy, and support requirements.
4. A documented contribution-margin advantage over centralized fallback capacity after payment, support, fraud, and payout costs.
5. Completed settlement, provider agreement, tax, sanctions, privacy, and security reviews.
6. Server-authoritative balances, reservations, receipts, fees, provider earnings, withdrawals, replay protection, and audit logs.

These are management gates, not forecasts or investor promises.

## Experimental pricing

Today's test defaults, such as a 20% platform fee and token-level input/output prices, are for experimental routing only. They are not validated market pricing and are not in the financial model. Launch pricing would be rebuilt from observed demand, costs, host payouts, fraud loss, and support burden, and the marketplace would launch only if it improves customer economics without lowering contribution margin below the Cloud target.

## Who decides what

The downloadable app is never the commercial authority. HivemindOS-controlled hosted infrastructure, or verifiable settlement rails, decide buyer balances, job settlement, provider eligibility and reputation, fees and payouts, refunds and disputes, payment verification, and private-workload eligibility.

The app may discover local models, set up an opt-in worker, show status, and display signed receipts. It cannot create balances, choose fee recipients, approve payouts, or override hosted policy.

## Launch decision

If the gates pass, Hive Compute becomes a second stage of the metered-execution business. If they do not, HivemindOS keeps buying capacity from established providers and stays focused on reliable managed execution.

# HivemindOS Cloud Agent Calls

Cloud Agent Calls are the paid, room-based version of agent voice. The free version already covers the common case: one person talking to one agent.

## Free versus paid

The free call path is BYOK: one user, one selected agent, the user's own OpenAI Realtime key, and a direct call from the dashboard or phone. See [Calling](../../for-users/features/calling.html).

A call with several people or several agents needs a real room, and a room needs managed infrastructure. HivemindOS creates the room, issues access tokens for each participant, brings the call agent in, keeps it running, and routes audio between everyone. That is an operated service, so it is paid.

| Capability                               | BYOK Agent Calls | HivemindOS Cloud Agent Calls |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------: | ---------------------------: |
| One user calls one selected agent        |         Included |                     Included |
| Uses user's OpenAI Realtime key directly |              Yes |                     Optional |
| Managed LiveKit rooms                    |               No |                          Yes |
| SFU audio routing                        |               No |                          Yes |
| Mobile-friendly room tokens              |               No |                          Yes |
| Multiple humans in one call              |               No |                          Yes |
| Multiple agents in one call              |               No |                          Yes |
| Hosted room reliability                  |               No |                          Yes |
| Premium paid service                     |               No |                          Yes |

The call agent can still hand work to the agent on your computer, so a room call is still an agent call, not a separate voice assistant.

## Why rooms need a server

Rooms use an SFU (selective forwarding unit): a switchboard server. Each participant sends audio once to the server, and the server forwards it to everyone else. Without one, every participant must connect directly to every other participant, which breaks down quickly. With one, a room can hold a user on phone and desktop, a friend, and several agents at once.

## Product promise

The promise is not "pay to talk to your agent." It is "pay when HivemindOS runs the room for you." Free BYOK calls stay the default. The room provider and voice-model credentials belong to the managed HivemindOS Cloud service, not to your local setup.

# Revenue Allocations And Treasury Boundaries

This is the canonical page for where HivemindOS revenue goes. There are two fixed allocations, one for HIVE buybacks and one for the company treasury. Neither is a staking-income program.

## The allocation

| Revenue rail | Allocation | Boundary |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| HIVE buybacks | 15% of the per-service allocation basis | Separate purchase rail; not funded by the company treasury |
| Company treasury | 15% of the per-service allocation basis | Company property; does not fund HIVE buybacks |
| Outside tokenomics allocation | Remainder of the per-service allocation basis | This policy assigns no fixed use |

The two 15% allocations are exact and separate. They are not one shared bucket, and the treasury is not the source of HIVE purchases.

## What the allocation is based on

Each service's allocation basis is server-owned and published in the official commercial catalog: realized margin for most managed services, recognized platform revenue for others.

Recognized platform revenue is narrower than what customers pay or total transaction volume:

- It excludes pass-through costs, refunds, and reversals.
- For marketplaces, only HivemindOS's earned fee counts, not gross merchandise value.
- Purchased credits count only when they are spent.

HivemindOS's hosted infrastructure calculates and records both allocations. The app, a local setting, a connected wallet, or a client revenue claim cannot choose the basis, amount, or recipient.

## HIVE buybacks

The buyback rail buys HIVE with its own 15% allocation. Confirmed purchases are published in the [HIVE Buyback Ledger](https://hivemindos.app/buybacks/). Pending allocations, failed batches, and unconfirmed transactions do not count.

HivemindOS does not burn HIVE. A buyback is not a distribution, staking payment, price floor, liquidity guarantee, or promise of appreciation.

## Company treasury

The treasury allocation is company property, and this policy does not fix how it is used. It gives HIVE holders and stakers no:

- ownership of company or treasury assets
- redemption right
- share of revenue
- automatic distribution
- binding governance or control over treasury decisions
- promise that treasury funds will buy HIVE

## Staking is separate

The staking vault holds user principal. It funds neither buybacks nor the treasury, and staking creates no claim on either. The six tiers give product benefits only; see [HIVE Staking And Community Tiers](hive-staking-and-community-tiers.html).

## Changes

Allocation definitions, percentages, timing, minimums, caps, and execution may change, pause, or end going forward for legal, regulatory, tax, accounting, corporate, security, liquidity, market-integrity, reserve, solvency, or operational reasons. Material changes are published with an effective date. Completed on-chain purchases stay on the public record. Holding or staking HIVE gives no contractual right to the policy continuing.
