Zero Human Companies

A Zero Human Company is a team of agents working toward one business goal, with you in charge of money, approvals, and whether it keeps running. Use it for repeatable work that should run like a small company rather than a one-off chat.

What a company is

Each company has:

  • a charter and one main goal (the “apex goal”);
  • a crew of agents, each with a role;
  • budgets, approval rules, and a kill switch that you control;
  • a cockpit where you watch its work, runs, and results.

The company runs on one of these engines:

Engine What happens
HivemindOS crew (default) HivemindOS breaks the goal into Work Board tasks and routes them to the crew. Crew members can be agents on your machines, always-on Hivemind Bots, or a mix.
Hivemind Bot · first-party One always-on Bot runs the whole company on a hosted schedule, with no Work Board tasks. It keeps working while your computers are off.
AEON background skill One chosen skill runs in a linked AEON workspace. See Use AEON With Zero Human Companies.
Grok Bot · external handoff HivemindOS prepares a reviewed company file and runbook. You set up and run the Bot yourself in Grok.

Pick the crew engine when you need several roles handing work to each other on the Work Board. Pick a first-party Bot when one always-on agent can own the whole company.

Create a company

  1. Open More → Companies and choose Create company.
  2. Describe the outcome the company exists to achieve.
  3. Review its charter, goal, crew, capabilities, budget, and approval rules.
  4. Keep the HivemindOS crew engine unless you have a reason to choose another.
  5. Create the company, check its cockpit, and launch only when the first plan and limits look right.

Creating a company does not spend money, publish anything, or start work.

For an outcome-first setup with ready-made templates, use Founder Mode instead. It builds a reviewable blueprint and can import a shared company template.

Import an existing project

You can turn an existing project into a company without starting over. Choose a repository folder, review what HivemindOS finds, and create the company. HivemindOS records the project’s workflows and schedules (for example GitHub Actions, cron jobs, and hosting schedules) and shows them in the Systems tab.

You can also import a data room: a folder of plans, reports, spreadsheets, and other company documents. HivemindOS converts them on your machine and lists them in the Sources tab. Imported documents are treated as untrusted reference material. They cannot approve spending, launch work, or become standing instructions. See Documents And Brain Drop for supported file types.

Assign agents

One agent identity belongs to one company at a time. The company assignment controls that agent’s company budget, daily spend cap, approvals, mailbox, and work history, so sharing it between companies would make those controls unclear.

To reuse the same agent in another company, choose Duplicate agent in the crew picker. The copy gets its own identity and wallet but can keep the same model, runtime, skills, and personality. You choose whether to copy its environment, private memory, or chat history.

If an identity ends up in more than one company anyway, spending stops for it until you remove it from all but one.

Launch and stop

The Board tab has one launch control. What it does depends on the engine:

  • HivemindOS crew: plans the goal into Work Board tasks. Later cycles wait until the board is idle and a crew member is free.
  • Hivemind Bot: runs one cycle and sets up a hosted schedule on the Bot.
  • AEON: sends the goal to the chosen skill. Later cycles wait until that workspace is idle.
  • Grok Bot: there is no launch button. Prepare the handoff and set up the Bot in Grok.

To launch, a company needs a goal and an unfrozen treasury. The crew engine also needs at least one agent or running Bot; the Bot engine needs a running Bot with managed credit.

Stop autonomy stops new work; work already in progress finishes. Freeze stops company spending. Launching also makes the machine you pressed it on the company’s home machine, so only that machine dispatches work.

Cadence. Existing companies repeat every 30 minutes by default. The always-on watcher templates start at 60 minutes. Shorten the interval only after results and provider limits justify it.

A stopped Bot stays on the crew but gets no work until you start it again in More → Hivemind Bots. Bot work uses your managed-agent credit.

Budgets and approvals

These controls govern the company’s native work:

  • Treasury — budgets, spending, per-agent caps, recorded revenue, and the kill switch.
  • Approvals — spending and actions waiting for your sign-off.
  • Spend & limits — daily or monthly request and spend limits per provider or per operation.

Limits stack: a call goes ahead only if every limit that applies allows it. HivemindOS reserves the expected cost before a call, so a retry is not counted twice, and a frozen company is blocked before the call. Removing a limit keeps its history.

For Google Cloud, the same tab can find your projects, APIs, and billing accounts and set daily quota caps and a monthly billing budget. Daily quotas are hard caps enforced by Google. Billing budgets only send alerts; they do not shut anything off. Raising an existing limit asks you to confirm twice.

Some things are not covered by company budgets:

  • AEON workspaces and Grok Bots use their own credentials and limits. Set spending limits with those providers. Grok Bots on one account share the same computer, files, and logins, so they are not separate security boundaries.
  • A first-party Bot’s company budget is guidance, not payment authority. Unattended hosted spending needs a separately governed wallet or provider rail.
  • External tools are not intercepted automatically.

Limits are local controls and estimates. Your provider’s billing records remain the source of truth.

Runs and proposals

The Runs tab records each important step the company takes: dispatches, task outcomes, preview reviews, revenue events, and replay requests. Each run keeps its input, status, events, and outputs.

Proposals are decisions waiting for you, such as a price change, a blocker that needs your input, a customer-preview decision, or a replay. Each proposal is marked pending, applied, rejected, or superseded, so you have a record of what the company suggested and what you chose.

A replay never redoes customer-facing or money-facing work silently. It creates a proposal, and you choose when to go ahead.

Results

  • Deliverables shows what the company actually produced, such as live sites, published pieces, and videos. Scratch files and receipts go into a collapsed work log.
  • Comms shows outreach threads and the crew’s mailboxes. It appears for companies that do outreach, and reads mail from AgentMail and Cloudflare Agentic Inbox. Create mailboxes in agent settings; see Apps And Agent Providers. If nothing is connected or no mail has arrived, the tab says why.
  • Sales and Products show customers, pipeline, and fixed products for companies that sell.
  • Analytics shows company performance. It can read a site from Hivemind Analytics or your own analytics account.

Every company task needs evidence before it counts as done. Product, design, content, and customer-facing work also needs review by a different agent. If evidence or a reviewer is missing, the task stops for your review.

Over time the company builds capability capital: reusable workflows, checks, receipts, and reviewed lessons that stay with the company even when its agents or models change. This is a summary of learned capability, not a money balance. Skill improvements the company suggests always need your approval before they are installed. See Agent Evaluations.

Connect bookings and CRM

  • Bookings. Company agents can manage booking pages and appointments through Cal.com, Cal.diy, or Managed Hivemind Bookings. Each call must belong to an active company task, and changes need your confirmation. See Agent-Managed Bookings.
  • CRM. Assign a Twenty workspace in the company’s Connections tab. Changes need an active task and your confirmation. See Twenty CRM.

Revenue Recording And Platform Fees

You can record revenue a company earns elsewhere, by amount and source. Revenue earned outside HivemindOS is not charged. Importing an existing project does not make its past or outside revenue subject to a fee.

A fee can apply only to a transaction HivemindOS itself supplies, bills, or hosts, and only under a disclosed policy enforced by HivemindOS servers. The app and manually recorded revenue cannot create a fee.

Record revenue automatically

Revenue can also arrive in the company ledger on its own. Each payment is counted once, even if it is re-sent.

  • x402 product offers. Publish a product from the company catalog as a paid x402 endpoint. The server always charges the catalog price, and each settled purchase is recorded for the company. This follows the same self-hosted seller opt-in as the paid-agent gateway (HIVEMINDOS_PAID_AGENT_SELLER_MODE=self-hosted).
  • Stripe. Point a Stripe webhook at POST /api/company-revenue/stripe-webhook, and set metadata.companyId (or client_reference_id) on the payment link or checkout session. Set HIVEMINDOS_COMPANY_STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET (or STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET). Only the settled amount is recorded.

For money goals, recorded revenue updates the company’s goal progress. The Treasury tab shows whether each revenue source is connected, and why not if it isn’t.

Advanced: Frontier Lab

Frontier Lab lets one small crew run more work in parallel, with every task reviewed and every token accounted for. You don’t need it to run a company. It requires the HivemindOS crew engine with the hierarchical process.

Models. It uses a fixed three-tier ladder through your OpenAI sign-in (OAuth), with no fallback to other providers:

  • Scout — gpt-5.6-luna: research, triage, and planning.
  • Builder — gpt-5.6-terra: building, operations, and deployment.
  • Reviewer — gpt-5.6-sol: independent review and QA.

You need a working OpenAI sign-in and at least two separate agents, so no agent reviews its own work.

Token budget. You set a monthly token ceiling. Before each task runs, HivemindOS reserves its token amount; afterward it records what was actually used. If usage can’t be measured, the full reservation is counted. Overages cut capacity to zero until the month resets or you raise the ceiling.

Scale stages. You choose the stage. Moving up requires proven results:

Stage Maximum parallel tasks Tasks per cycle Turns per machine Evidence required to enter
Pilot 4 4 1 Starting stage
Team 12 12 2 3 settled tasks at 67% success
Frontier 24 24 4 12 settled tasks at 80% success

Scaling down is always allowed. Scaling up is never automatic, and stages can’t be skipped.

Earned Scale. The cockpit compares your current stage with the one below it across outcome, proof, speed, tokens, unique contribution, duplication, human intervention, and reviewer disagreement. It needs at least three runs at each stage. It then recommends Scale, Hold, Reduce, or Collect evidence. A proof rate below 90% or any proof regression means Reduce. Moving from Team to Frontier requires a Scale result. The recommendation never changes your settings or spends tokens on its own.

To set it up, open a company, choose Frontier Lab, pick a token ceiling and limits, choose a stage, and save.
